# Paper Rally visual roadmap

## Four rally games, four lessons

This review draws on the developers’ public descriptions and store material. The proposals below are original designs for Paper Rally’s folded geometry, printed inks and Android cameras.

| Reference | What to take from it | Where it matters in Paper Rally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [art of rally](https://store.steampowered.com/app/550320/art_of_rally/) | A stylised location and a car from a particular era can each have an unmistakable silhouette. A big shape often carries further than lettering. | Give each route an approach, reveal and departure. Compare cars from chase height in one neutral paint colour. |
| [Pocket Rally](https://www.illusion-magic.com/pr) | Its developer highlights mountains, coastline, ancient ruins and replay cameras chosen for each track. A camera position can be part of designing a landmark. | Frame a landmark through the windscreen on approach, then find a second angle that shows the car crossing it. |
| [Rush Rally 3](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2020860/Rush_Rally_3/) | Night, rain, snow and real-time deformation tie a car’s appearance to what happened during the run. | Put evidence of slip, water depth and the point of impact where players can see it from their usual camera. |
| [Rush Rally Origins](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1728660/Rush_Rally_Origins/) | Top-down stages combine changing light and weather with distinct snow, gravel, dirt, mud and tarmac surfaces. | Test surface boundaries, crests and roadside clutter from the high camera as well as at 58° in the cockpit. |

## First batch to prototype

| Priority | Improvement | In-game review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Give the three modern hatchbacks different rear openings. Use a short upright hatch on Rally2, a broader rear screen on WRC and a deeply recessed opening beneath Rally1’s twin wing. | Hide liveries and labels. Identify each car from the default chase distance. |
| 2 | Give Slate Gorge three geological sections. Start in loose scree, enter vertically split slate at the first crossing, then expose pale bands above the last ravine. | Three unlabelled screenshots should reveal their order along the route. |
| 3 | Shape the two approaches to an Alpine col. Open a long uphill view to the chairlift, then let distant peaks disappear behind the shoulder on the descent. | The first tower should become visible before the driver needs to brake. |
| 4 | Give damaged parts material-specific behaviour. Fold a painted wing, crease a door along its seam and split a lamp insert at the struck corner. | Photograph the same front-quarter impact on a classic and a modern car. The contact point should be evident in each. |
| 5 | Break a river’s flow around fixed objects. Print a dark wedge upstream of each pier, lighter paired wakes downstream and quiet water behind the abutment. | At rest, highlights should travel with the river and stay attached to the correct pier. |
| 6 | Distinguish compacted snow from polished ice with a broken blue-grey strip on braking approaches. Match its extent to the lower-grip patch. | From the cockpit the driver should see the transition before steering onto it. |
| 7 | Add landmark-specific photo viewpoints at the quarry conveyor, stone arch and harbour slipway. Position them on the public side of barriers and remember the player’s previous camera. | A single camera selection should show the whole car against the landmark. |
| 8 | Tie loose-surface effects to individual tyre contacts. An inside wheel clipping gravel should produce a short, one-sided fan. | Drive with two wheels on tarmac and two on the verge, then pause in photo mode. |

## Car exteriors and cockpits

1. **Wheel openings.** Give the narrow classics pressed semicircular lips, Group B cars bolt-on box flares and modern cars vented trailing edges. Check for tyre penetration at full lock and maximum suspension compression.
2. **Wheel faces.** Add a shallow dished steel wheel, a closed gravel wheel and an open tarmac wheel. Keep their hubs identifiable while spinning slowly in a replay.
3. **Glass depth.** Recess rear glass inside a visible rubber seal on older cars. Give modern bonded glass a flush printed border that remains legible on dark paint.
4. **Boot hardware.** Fit era-specific external hinges, quick-release straps or recessed handles. Reserve the largest details for cars with little other rear ornament.
5. **Mechanical packaging.** Let rear-engined cars show their cooling paths through intake or exhaust placement. Avoid identical vents on every car.
6. **Underbody.** Make a folded skid plate visible beneath the nose on a crest. Limit transmission and exhaust detail to surfaces visible during jumps or in photo mode.
7. **Livery registration.** Continue broad stripes around the corner of the boot, with a small break at opening panels. Test all three paints before approving a body.
8. **Crew compartment.** Show a suspended intercom cable and a bound pace-note book on the co-driver’s side. Position both outside the apex sightline.
9. **Night instruments.** Vary the printed dial backlight and shift lamp housing by era. Check readability in darkness and in a bright snow stage at default FOV.
10. **Service history.** Add a slightly different ink tone on a replaced wing and discrete fasteners after repair. Make that state deterministic for photo and replay.

## Surface, weather and motion

1. **Gravel braking zones.** Push loose stones towards the outside of repeated braking lines. Drive past at race speed to check they read as texture rather than obstacles.
2. **Ford entry and exit.** Shape the entry splash around the front tyres and give the exit a shorter trailing sheet from the rear tyres. A stationary car should cease throwing spray.
3. **Drying cobbles.** Let raised cobble crowns lighten before the joints. Match the wet edge to the adjacent gutter on Harbour Lanes.
4. **Snowbank contact.** Eject a short burst of irregular pale fragments at the struck corner and leave a shallow notch for the duration of the run.
5. **Canopy rain.** Change from fine open rainfall to occasional heavier drops at the forest edge. Use a small recycled emitter pool.
6. **Wind agreement.** Use one stage wind direction for flags, smoke, snow drift and grass. Check direction at a switchback where the car reverses its heading.
7. **Dust in shade.** Cool and darken dust under forest cover, then warm it in a clearing. Preserve the silhouette of the following car.
8. **Landing response.** Compress suspension and mudflaps on the wheels that touch first. Compare a diagonal landing with a level one in slow motion.
9. **Bridge acoustics.** Add a brief timber thump, steel-joint tick or enclosed reflection at the matching wheel or portal crossing. Base its pitch and spacing on vehicle speed.
10. **Mobile review.** Record the same night bridge, wet forest and four-car start on a target handset. Compare frame times, light stability and memory before increasing scenery density.

For the next playable build, start with the hatchback silhouettes, Slate Gorge strata and tyre-specific gravel. Each has a clear acceptance check and remains visible on a small screen during ordinary driving.
